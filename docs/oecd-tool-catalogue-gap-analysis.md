# OECD Catalogue — Governance Gap Analysis

## Purpose

This note highlights a useful distinction:

public trustworthy-AI tool catalogues can be rich in assurance techniques while
remaining thinner in deeper governance design surfaces.

That is not necessarily a flaw in the catalogue.

It is a signal about where the current ecosystem is mature and where it is
still structurally weak.

## Likely tool-rich areas

The OECD catalogue appears structurally well-suited to surface public resources
for:

- bias and fairness tooling
- transparency and documentation
- robustness and security
- evaluation and benchmarking
- auditing methods
- awareness, training, and reskilling

These are important, and they matter.

They represent real mitigation supply.

## Likely governance-thin areas

The catalogue is less likely to solve, by itself, problems such as:

- delegated accountability
- legal principal binding
- runtime policy authority
- dispute-grade evidence
- portability governance
- doctrine-level policy coupling
- upstream normative framing

Those are not simply “missing tool” problems.

They are often:

- institutional design problems
- authority-design problems
- lifecycle-governance problems
- policy architecture problems

## Why this matters

Without making this distinction, a repository can accidentally imply that a
public catalogue of tools is equivalent to a public catalogue of governance
solutions.

It is not.

A tool-rich environment may still be governance-poor.

That is one reason this repository separates:

- failure classes
- anti-patterns
- constructive patterns
- external mitigation supply

## Practical use

This gap analysis can be used in two ways:

### 1. To identify realistic mitigation support

Where the OECD catalogue is strong, crosswalk it to:

- likely mitigation families
- supporting tools or methods
- procedural reinforcement

### 2. To identify structural supply gaps

Where the OECD catalogue is thin, treat that as a public finding:

- the market may have many assurance tools
- but still lack mature governance design patterns in critical areas

## Working principle

The question is not:

> are there tools?

The better question is:

> are the available tools addressing the real governance weakness, or only its
> more visible technical symptoms?
